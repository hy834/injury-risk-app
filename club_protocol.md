# Riverside Rovers: Medical and Load-Management Protocol (fictional)

One clause per line. The app reads every line that starts with [P. Edit the text to change the app's briefings.

[P1] Load management: if the acute:chronic workload ratio is above 1.5, reduce the player's next session load by 20 percent and flag the player for medical review.
[P2] Load management: a workload ratio between 1.3 and 1.5 is a caution zone. Keep the planned load but monitor soreness and sleep daily for the next three sessions.
[P3] Sleep: fewer than 6 hours average sleep over the last week requires a medical team check-in before the next high-intensity session.
[P4] Soreness: a self-reported soreness score of 7 or above on the 0 to 10 scale requires a physiotherapy assessment before training resumes.
[P5] Recovery markers: a heart-rate variability reading more than 15 percent below the player's personal baseline is a recovery warning. Combine it with other flags before changing training.
[P6] Prior injury: a player injured in the last 12 months returns to full load only after a graded return-to-play plan signed by the medical team.
[P7] Conflicting signals: if the risk model says High but no load, sleep or soreness rule is triggered, the medical team reviews the player manually. Do not reduce load on the model score alone.
[P8] Clearance: only a member of the medical team may clear a player for full training or match selection. No automated system may clear or bench a player.
[P9] Documentation: every flag, decision and sign-off is recorded with the date, the staff member and the protocol clause used.
[P10] Football: sprint load drives hamstring injuries, so for football players the workload ratio caution zone starts at 1.2 and the 20 percent load reduction applies above 1.4.
[P11] Cricket: for cricket players, a workload ratio above 1.3 caps bowling volume in the next session at 75 percent of plan. Batting and fielding load stay as planned.
[P12] Basketball: jump load stresses knees and ankles, so for basketball players a soreness score of 6 or above requires a physiotherapy assessment before jumping drills.
[P13] Kabaddi: kabaddi is a contact sport, so a kabaddi player injured in the last 12 months does no full-contact raiding or tackling drills until cleared under the return-to-play plan.
[P14] Precedence: when a sport-specific clause and a general clause give different thresholds, the sport-specific clause applies. If the case is still unclear, the medical team decides.
